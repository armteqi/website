1. What is Responsible Disclosure?

Responsible Disclosure means privately reporting a security issue to Armteqi before sharing it publicly. This gives us time to investigate and resolve the issue without putting our community at unnecessary risk.

We believe that working with the security community—rather than against it—makes everyone safer.

2. Systems Covered

This policy applies to systems and services directly operated by Armteqi:

  • Armteqi Website – The main website at armteqi.org and all its subdomains.
  • GitHub Organization – Public repositories under the Armteqi organization.
  • Trust Center – The trust.armteqi.org subdomain and related pages.
  • Official Community Platforms – Community channels directly administered by Armteqi (e.g., WhatsApp groups, Discord servers).
  • Future Services – Any new services operated directly by Armteqi will be covered by this policy.

Note: Third-party platforms (e.g., GitHub itself, WhatsApp's infrastructure) are not covered by this policy. Please report vulnerabilities in those platforms directly to their respective providers.

3. How to Report

To report a security issue, email us at armteqi.community@gmail.com with:

  • Subject: Security Report
  • Description: A clear explanation of the vulnerability.
  • Steps: Step-by-step instructions to reproduce the issue.
  • Screenshots: Visual evidence (if applicable).
  • Proof of Concept: Code or a script to demonstrate the vulnerability (if possible).
  • Impact: The potential security impact of the vulnerability.

Please do not share the vulnerability publicly until we have had a chance to investigate and address it.

4. Responsible Testing Guidelines

Good-faith researchers should follow these principles:

  • Avoid damaging or deleting data.
  • Avoid accessing or modifying personal information.
  • Avoid disrupting services for other users.
  • Test only what is necessary to confirm the vulnerability.
  • Keep findings confidential until the issue is resolved.

5. Out of Scope

The following types of issues are considered out of scope for this policy:

  • Social engineering (e.g., phishing, pretexting).
  • Spam or bulk messaging.
  • Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks.
  • Physical attacks (e.g., physical access to hardware).
  • Vulnerabilities in third-party platforms (e.g., vulnerabilities in WhatsApp, Discord, or GitHub's infrastructure).
  • Rate limiting issues.
  • Missing security headers without demonstrated impact.
  • Self-XSS (Cross-Site Scripting that affects only the user performing the test).
  • Clickjacking without sensitive actions (e.g., no account takeover or data modification).

6. What We Will Do

When you report a vulnerability to us, we commit to the following:

  • Acknowledge: We will acknowledge valid reports as soon as reasonably possible.
  • Investigate: We will investigate the issue and determine its impact.
  • Inform: We will keep you informed of our progress when appropriate.
  • Resolve: We will work to resolve the issue as quickly as we can.
  • Thank: We may publicly thank researchers after the issue is resolved, unless they request to remain anonymous.

7. Recognition

Armteqi currently does not offer financial rewards (bug bounties) for vulnerability reports.

However, we may acknowledge researchers who responsibly report valid security issues, unless they request to remain anonymous. Recognition may include:

  • A mention in our security acknowledgments page.
  • A public thank-you in our community channels.
  • Credit in the disclosure of the resolved issue.

We believe that recognition, combined with the satisfaction of making the internet safer, is a meaningful way to thank our contributors.

8. Safe Harbor

If you act in good faith, follow this policy, avoid causing harm, and give us reasonable time to investigate, Armteqi will view your research as a good-faith effort to improve security.

We will not take legal action against researchers who comply with these guidelines and act responsibly. We appreciate your help in making Armteqi safer for everyone.
