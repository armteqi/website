Cyber Ethics

Cyber Ethics Framework

Technology is most powerful when it is used responsibly. This framework defines the ethical principles that guide every member, volunteer, contributor, and learner within the Armteqi community.

Version 1.0 Effective July 2026 Applies to All Members
Technology Creates Opportunity — Ethics Creates Trust.

At Armteqi, we believe cybersecurity is not simply about finding vulnerabilities or mastering technical skills. It is about protecting people, respecting privacy, acting responsibly, and using knowledge to strengthen society.

Every member is expected to uphold these principles whenever representing Armteqi, participating in community activities, conducting research, or interacting online.

Protect People

Security knowledge must always be used to protect individuals, organizations, and communities.

Respect Privacy

Personal information deserves confidentiality, dignity, and responsible handling.

Act Responsibly

Every action should reflect honesty, accountability, and professional integrity.

01 Purpose

Cybersecurity provides the ability to understand, identify, and improve digital systems. That knowledge carries significant responsibility.

The purpose of this framework is to establish a shared ethical standard for every member of Armteqi. These principles guide our decisions regardless of experience level, role, or technical ability.

Our Mission
Use technology to educate, protect, collaborate, and create positive impact.

02 Core Ethical Principles

Every decision within Armteqi should be measured against these principles.

Respect

03 Member Responsibilities

Every member contributes to the reputation of Armteqi. Our actions—online and offline—should demonstrate professionalism, respect, and accountability.

Think before you act. Ask yourself: "Will this action help people, improve security, and reflect Armteqi's values?"

04 Respect & Privacy

Every individual deserves dignity, privacy, and equal treatment regardless of experience, nationality, gender, background, or technical ability.

Respecting privacy is not only a legal obligation— it is an ethical responsibility.

05 Authorization Before Testing

Testing or probing systems without explicit permission is unacceptable. Authorization must be written and clearly define scope, duration, and allowed methods.

Respect organizational policies. Even with permission, never exceed defined boundaries. Scope limitations protect both the tester and the organization being tested.

Good Practice
Always obtain written authorization before testing. Define clear scope and adhere to it strictly. Communicate with the system owner throughout the process.
Never Do
Never scan, probe, or access systems you do not own or have explicit permission to test. Unauthorized testing is unethical and often illegal.

06 Ethical Security Research

Research should always serve a constructive purpose—to advance knowledge, strengthen defenses, or improve security practices.

Never conduct research with the intent to cause harm, expose data, or disrupt services. Use safe lab environments and isolated test networks for practical experimentation.

Example
A member sets up a controlled virtual lab to test common vulnerabilities. They document their findings to share best practices with the community, never targeting live systems without explicit authorization.

07 Responsible Vulnerability Disclosure

If you discover a vulnerability, follow a structured disclosure process. Verify the issue, report it privately to the affected party, and allow reasonable time for remediation.

Public disclosure should only occur after coordination with the vendor or owner. This practice protects users and upholds professional standards.

For detailed guidelines, refer to the Responsible Disclosure Policy.

08 Ethical Use of Artificial Intelligence

AI is a powerful learning tool. Use it to understand concepts faster, generate practice problems, or automate routine tasks—but never to generate malware, phishing content, or fraud.

Always verify AI-generated information. The output of AI models is not always accurate. Human judgment remains the ultimate responsibility.

Best Practice
Treat AI as a junior assistant—review its work critically. Do not share sensitive or private data with public AI tools.

09 Knowledge Sharing

Armteqi thrives on open, responsible knowledge sharing. Teach beginners, write guides, and participate in discussions.

Always credit original authors, respect licenses (MIT, GPL, and others), and never share tools or techniques that could be misused without appropriate context and safeguards.

Key Principle
Knowledge grows when it is shared. Gatekeeping weakens the community. We rise together.

10 Community Commitment

Every member represents Armteqi. Our reputation is built not by our technical abilities alone, but by our integrity, humility, professionalism, and willingness to protect others.

Commit to learning responsibly, treating others with dignity, and using knowledge to strengthen the digital ecosystem. When you succeed, you elevate the entire community.

Our Shared Pledge
I will use technology to educate, protect, and empower. I will act with integrity. I will respect privacy. I will represent Armteqi with professionalism.

11 Reporting Ethical Concerns

If you observe unethical behavior, report it. All reports are treated confidentially and with care.

Armteqi does not tolerate retaliation against good-faith reporters. Speaking up protects the community and upholds our shared values.

Cybersecurity begins with ethics.

Technical knowledge without responsibility can create harm. Technical knowledge guided by integrity creates trust, protects people, and strengthens the digital world.

Every member of Armteqi is expected to uphold these principles whenever they learn, teach, research, or contribute.
